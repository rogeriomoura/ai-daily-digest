# AI Daily Digest — 2026-11-22

Another quiet day for brand-new model drops; today's picks lean on a DeepMind rollout milestone and three developing "other news" stories — an Amazon acquisition in talks, a second Fireworks AI breach disclosure, and new specifics on Jensen Huang's Congressional testimony.

## Model Releases
- **Google DeepMind rolls out Genie 4 to Project Genie for AI Ultra subscribers** — Genie 4, DeepMind's real-time interactive "world model" first unveiled October 27, began reaching eligible Google AI Ultra subscribers in Project Genie on November 18, per Google's own blog and multiple outlets (LetsDataScience, PPC Land, AIWeekly). It adds native audio — ambient sound, music, and interactive dialogue generated alongside the simulated world — plus mid-session scene edits; Google still calls it an early, experimental research preview, with no published parameter count and reports disagreeing on exact session-length limits (roughly 2–3 minutes). [Source](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/models-and-research/google-deepmind/project-genie/)

## Other News
- **Amazon reportedly nearing a ~$7–7.5B deal to acquire AI startup Decart** — Per TechRepublic (citing Calcalist and Globes) and corroborating reports, Amazon is in advanced talks to buy Decart, which makes real-time generative-video tools (Lucy) and simulated-environment training tools (Oasis) along with chip-efficiency software that could benefit Amazon's Trainium chips. The deal isn't officially confirmed by either company; Decart was valued near $4B in May, and Anthropic had separately explored acquiring it before dropping talks in September after due diligence. [Source](https://www.techrepublic.com/article/news-amazon-decart-ai-acquisition-talks/)
- **Fireworks AI discloses a second breach, this one touching customer datasets and billing data** — Nvidia-backed inference provider Fireworks AI told customers November 9 that attackers who compromised a third-party cloud provider hosting its R&D systems (around October 23–24) used that access to reach Fireworks development machines — a separate incident from an October 6 breach tied to stolen internal credentials. Per The Record and BleepingComputer, exposed data reportedly includes billing records, invoices, and some customer datasets (Notion says it affected about 1% of its own customers); Fireworks says no payment-card numbers were taken and it found no evidence attackers touched customer models. [Source](https://therecord.media/ai-company-fireworks-says-hackers-stole-data-in-oct-attacks)
- **Jensen Huang agrees to closed-door Congressional testimony December 1** — Updating the still-unfolding Nvidia leadership fallout covered here in recent days: per TechCrunch and CNBC, Huang — who resigned as Nvidia CEO November 12 amid affair and foreign-intelligence-contact allegations — agreed to answer questions privately to House committees on December 1 after three Republican-led committees threatened to subpoena him; Nvidia general counsel Tim Teter is separately set to appear December 2. Senate Banking Chair Tim Scott has pushed for Huang and Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick to testify as well, warning of his own subpoena if they don't cooperate (reports differ on the exact Senate date). [Source](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/12/jensen-huang-agrees-to-testify-before-congress/)
