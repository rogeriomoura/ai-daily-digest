# AI Daily Digest — 2026-11-18

A quieter day for new model drops, but a busier one for AI-adjacent security and policy news: a supply-chain breach hit a tampered Claude Code extension distributed through Cursor's marketplace, the FBI warned about an open-source AI hacking framework seeing real-world abuse, and the Trump administration is reportedly preparing to name AI "national champions."

## Open Source
- **Thinking Machines Lab publishes Inkling 2 Small's weights under Apache 2.0** — First unveiled November 5 with weights still closed (covered here November 10), Thinking Machines Lab put the full 594B-parameter, 17B-active MoE model's weights on Hugging Face on November 12, per multiple outlets (AlphaSignal, Let's Data Science, Hermes AI). Two variants are listed (NVFP4/MXFP8 by one account, differently labeled by another); at least one report flags that the repository's model-card metadata declares Apache 2.0 but lacks an actual LICENSE/NOTICE file, so downstream users should check the Hugging Face page directly before relying on the license. [Source](https://alphasignal.ai/news/thinking-machines-opens-inkling-2-small-s-594b-weights-to-developers)

## Other News
- **Cursor confirms a supply-chain breach planted a trojanized Claude Code extension in its marketplace** — Disclosed in Cursor's own incident report around November 13 and reported by BleepingComputer, GovInfoSecurity, and others, attackers used a credential stolen via a breach at AI infra provider Fireworks AI to publish a tampered build (reported as v2.1.313, some outlets say v2.1.312) of Anthropic's Claude Code extension on Cursor's VS Code marketplace. The malicious build — not made by Anthropic — tried to plant an SSH backdoor on affected machines; Cursor says exposure was capped at roughly 5,000 users over an ~11-hour window on October 29, and it has since rotated keys and pushed users to v2.1.314. [Source](https://www.bleepingcomputer.com/news/security/cursor-confirms-security-breach-led-to-malicious-claude-code-extension/)
- **FBI issues a FLASH advisory on ARTEX, an open-source AI agent framework being used to automate cyberattacks** — Issued November 13 (reference FLASH-20261113-001) and reported by The Record, BleepingComputer, and others, the advisory says threat actors have used ARTEX — which connects to external LLMs (the FBI specifically flags DeepSeek's Flash family, though it also works with Claude and GPT) — since at least August 2026 against financial, government, defense, and healthcare targets, in some cases reaching privileged access in under three hours. The advisory also ties ARTEX to a ConnectWise supply-chain breach affecting Microsoft 365 customers, and notes that although the original developer pulled the repo, forks and a paid "ARTEX PRO" version remain available; Censys reportedly found over 1,600 exposed ARTEX consoles. [Source](https://therecord.media/artex-ai-tool-fbi-flash-alert)
- **WSJ: Trump administration plans to name AI "national champions"** — Reported November 15 by the Wall Street Journal and widely picked up (TheNextWeb, Finimize, and others), the plan would designate more than two dozen US AI companies — reportedly including Nvidia, Microsoft, Amazon, OpenAI, and Anthropic — as "national champions" to get fast-tracked export licenses, diplomatic support, and possible federal funding help selling abroad. Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick is expected to unveil the list, but timing and the full roster are unconfirmed, and Nvidia's expected inclusion is drawing criticism given separate, unrelated scrutiny of CEO Jensen Huang. [Source](https://thenextweb.com/news/ai-national-champions-nvidia-openai-anthropic-trump)
